MASCOT PLACEMENT AND MOVEMENT
  The mascot is a participant in the scene, not a corner watermark. Draw it
  standing on the scene's ground plane with a soft grey elliptical shadow under
  its feet grounding it.

  It TRAVELS OFTEN. Give it a new position for NEARLY EVERY SCENE, not just
  3 or 4 times across the whole video - by default, assume it moves at every
  scene boundary unless two consecutive scenes are so tightly linked that
  staying put is clearly more natural. Restlessness is the goal: the mascot
  should read as constantly repositioning itself to be near whatever the
  video is talking about right now, not settling into a small handful of
  fixed spots. The wide frame gives it more room than a vertical one, so let
  it cross real distance horizontally - stepping toward the illustration it
  points at, moving aside to make room for the next scene's metaphor. Each
  move is a smooth ease-in-out glide, 1.2-2s long, covering real distance
  across the frame - this is a walk, not a snap.
  Start near-static for the first 15% of the glide, then ease in to a steady
  pace, then ease out to a clean stop. Do not cut the glide short at 0.4-0.8s
  - that reads as a jump, not a walk.

  BETWEEN MOVES IT IS COMPLETELY STILL. No idle bob, no sway, no breathing,
  no float, no wobble, no weight-shift, no jitter, no continuous motion of any
  kind. When it arrives at a position it holds that position cleanly - this is
  a hard requirement, not a preference - idle motion has been rejected.

  ONE REACTION GESTURE PER SCENE IS ALLOWED, AND ONLY ONE. When a scene's
  headline, a number, or a new element on screen actually appears mid-scene
  (not at the scene's start), the mascot may do exactly one quick hop or
  head-tilt - a displacement of 40-100px that returns to the exact same spot,
  lasting 0.3-0.4s total - timed to land on that appearance. This is a single
  discrete beat, not a loop: before it, the mascot is still; after it
  completes and returns to its start position, the mascot is still again for
  the rest of the scene. Never combine a reaction gesture with a scene-to-
  scene glide in the same beat, and never repeat it more than once per scene.
  If nothing new appears mid-scene, skip the gesture entirely and stay still -
  it is not required every scene.
